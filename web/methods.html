Methods

Everything that turns the video into the sound, in the order it happens. Frame counts are at the clips' frame rate, $f_s = 29.99$ fps; audio runs at $F_s = 44100$ Hz. Source files are named in each section.

1. Pose dance_pose.py · track

MediaPipe Pose (BlazePose, model_complexity=1, its own temporal smoothing on, detection and tracking confidence 0.5), one pass per frame. For each of 33 landmarks $j$ in frame $t$ we keep the image position, normalised to $[0,1]$, and a visibility $\nu_{t,j}\in[0,1]$. Positions are converted to pixels: $$\mathbf p_{t,j} = (x_{t,j}\,W,\; y_{t,j}\,H),\qquad W\times H = 576\times1024,$$ with $y$ pointing down. Everything below is 2D image motion. Motion toward or away from the camera is not seen. (MediaPipe also gives hip-centred 3D “world” landmarks. They are used by the older vocabulary/sonify work, not here, because they remove travel across the floor and their depth is noisy.)

2. Cleaning motion.py · clean

Each coordinate series $p_j(t)$ (one per joint and axis) goes through these steps in order:

  1. Visibility gate. $p_{t,j} \leftarrow \text{NaN}$ if $\nu_{t,j} < 0.5$.
  2. Hampel filter (glitch removal). With a window of $\pm h$ frames, $h=4$ (9 frames ≈ 0.3 s): $$m_t = \operatorname{median}_{|k|\le h} p_{t+k},\qquad \sigma_t = 1.4826\,\operatorname{median}_{|k|\le h}|p_{t+k}-m_t|$$ $$p_t \leftarrow \text{NaN}\quad\text{if}\quad |p_t - m_t| > 3\max(\sigma_t,\,1\,\text{px}).$$ The 1 px floor stops sub-pixel noise from counting as an outlier when the joint is still.
  3. Gap fill. NaNs are linearly interpolated. Confidence is $c_{t,j}=1$ on observed frames and in gaps of $\le 8$ frames (0.3 s), and $0$ inside longer gaps. For a joint, $c$ is the minimum over its two axes, then box-smoothed over 5 frames, so it ramps in and out rather than switching.
  4. Low-pass. 4th-order Butterworth, cutoff $f_c = 5$ Hz (normalised $W_n = f_c/(f_s/2)$), run forwards and backwards (filtfilt). This gives zero phase, with an effective order of 8 and magnitude response $|H|^2$. Offline, so it costs no latency. Human limb motion sits mostly below 5 Hz, and tracker jitter mostly above.

Scale. Distances are measured in torso lengths so the numbers do not depend on how far she is from the camera: $$\ell_t = \big\| \tfrac12(\mathbf p_{\text{Lsh}}+\mathbf p_{\text{Rsh}}) - \tfrac12(\mathbf p_{\text{Lhip}}+\mathbf p_{\text{Rhip}}) \big\|,$$ median-filtered over 149 frames (5 s), then clipped to $[0.6,\,1.4]\times\operatorname{median}_t \ell_t$, so a forward fold (where the projected torso shrinks) does not blow up the scale. Derivatives are taken in pixels and then divided by $\ell_t$. Dividing the positions first would turn changes in scale into fake velocity.

3. Kinematics motion.py · derivatives, features

Savitzky–Golay derivatives of the filtered positions. At each frame, a polynomial of degree $d$ is fitted by least squares to a window of $N$ frames, and its analytic derivative at the centre is used:

window $N$degree $d$
velocity $\mathbf v = \dot{\mathbf p}$7 (0.23 s)3
acceleration $\mathbf a = \ddot{\mathbf p}$11 (0.37 s)3
jerk $\mathbf j = \dddot{\mathbf p}$15 (0.50 s)4

For the five end effectors $e\in\{$head (nose), L/R wrist, L/R ankle$\}$: $$s_e = c_e\,\frac{\|\mathbf v_e\|}{\ell},\qquad \alpha_e = c_e\,\frac{\|\mathbf a_e\|}{\ell},\qquad \kappa_e = c_e\,\frac{\|\mathbf j_e\|}{\ell}$$ in torsos/s, torsos/s², torsos/s³. Joints we cannot see ($c_e = 0$) therefore contribute no motion.

Jitter floor. Each limb's resting reading is subtracted in quadrature, so noise that remains after cleaning does not make sound: $$s_e \leftarrow \sqrt{\max\!\big(0,\; s_e^2 - (P_{10}[s_e])^2\big)},$$ where $P_{10}$ is that limb's 10th percentile over the clip. (Quadrature, because independent noise adds in power.) This is applied to speed only, not to $\alpha$ or $\kappa$.

Pelvis (for travel and Weight): $\mathbf v_{\text{pel}} = \tfrac12(\mathbf v_{\text{Lhip}}+\mathbf v_{\text{Rhip}})/\ell$, with $s_{\text{pel}} = \|\mathbf v_{\text{pel}}\|$ and travel $= v_{\text{pel},x}$ (signed, screen left/right).

The diagnostic plot ("How the motion is cleaned") compares $s_{\text{Rwrist}}$ with the uncleaned $\|\nabla \mathbf p^{\text{raw}}\| f_s / \ell$ (central differences of the raw, only gap-filled, positions).

4. Normalisation motion.py · normalise

All three clips are pooled (same dancer, same studio), so a given level of “strong” means the same in each clip. For a feature $x$ with pooled percentiles $P_2, P_{98}$: $$\hat x = \operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\frac{x-P_2}{P_{98}-P_2},\,0,\,1\Big).$$ Exceptions: Space is already a ratio in $[0,1]$ and is used as-is. Travel is symmetric, $\hat x = (x+m)/(2m)$ with $m = P_{98}(|x|)$, so $0.5$ means not travelling. Limb speed is $\hat s_e = \operatorname{clip}(s_e/P_{98}(s),0,1)$, with $P_{98}$ pooled over all five effectors.

5. Laban features motion.py · features

These are computable approximations of Laban's Effort qualities, following the usual reading in the literature (Larboulette & Gibet, A review of computable expressive descriptors of human motion, MOCO 2015). $\mathcal B_n$ is a centred moving average over $n$ frames and $\mathcal M_n$ a centred moving maximum.

Weight (strong ↔ light): peak kinetic energy

$$E_t = \sum_{e} s_{e,t}^2 + 2\,s_{\text{pel},t}^2,\qquad \text{Weight} = \mathcal B_{8}\Big(\sqrt{\mathcal M_{14}(E)}\Big)$$

This is unit mass per effector, with the pelvis counted twice (the trunk is heavy). The window is 14 frames ≈ 0.47 s. The square root puts it back in speed units. The final 0.27 s average removes the steps that the moving max leaves.

Time (sudden ↔ sustained): acceleration

$$\text{Time} = \mathcal B_{14}\Big(\tfrac15\textstyle\sum_{e}\alpha_e\Big)$$

On these clips Weight and Time correlate at ≈ 0.94 (a strong move is also a fast-accelerating one). That is why Time is heard through discrete sudden events rather than as a second continuous control.

Flow (bound ↔ free): jerkiness

Raw jerk mostly tracks acceleration again, so we use jerk relative to acceleration: how fast the acceleration itself changes. The ratio has units of Hz. Over the four limbs $e\in\{$L/R wrist, L/R ankle$\}$: $$\text{Flow} = \mathcal B_{14}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal B_{14}\big(\sum_e \kappa_e\big)}{\mathcal B_{14}\big(\sum_e \alpha_e\big) + 4}\right)$$ The constant 4 torsos/s² stops a nearly still body (small $\alpha$, small $\kappa$) from reading as extremely jerky. It still correlates at ≈ 0.7 with Time, so it is the weakest of the four features.

Space (direct ↔ indirect): path straightness of the hands

Over a centred window of $2h+1 = 29$ frames (≈ 0.97 s), for each wrist $k$: $$L_k(t) = \sum_{|\tau|\le h} \frac{\|\mathbf v_k(t+\tau)\|}{f_s\,\ell},\qquad D_k(t) = \frac{\|\mathbf p_k(t+h)-\mathbf p_k(t-h)\|}{\ell},\qquad d_k = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(D_k/L_k,\,0,\,1\big)$$ $$\text{Space} = \frac{\sum_k d_k L_k}{\sum_k L_k}$$

$d_k=1$ means a straight line and $d_k\to0$ means a wandering or returning path. The hand that travels further gets more weight. When $\sum_k L_k \le 0.4$ torsos (the hands are barely moving), Space is undefined. It is linearly interpolated across those stretches, then smoothed with $\mathcal B_8$.

Shape: how much room the body takes

$$\text{Shape} = \mathcal B_5\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{area}\big(\operatorname{hull}\{\mathbf p_j\}_{j\in J}\big)}{\ell^2}\right)$$

$J$ = nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles (13 points). The hull is the 2D convex hull.

6. Sudden events motion.py · normalise

These are peaks of each limb's acceleration $\alpha_e$ (four limbs, not the head), found by scipy.signal.find_peaks with one threshold pooled over all clips and limbs:

  • height $\ge \theta = P_{85}(\alpha)$, prominence $\ge 0.5\,\theta$, at least 7 frames (0.23 s) apart on the same limb
  • strength $\;\sigma = \min\!\Big(1,\; \dfrac{\alpha_{\text{peak}}-\theta}{P_{99.5}(\alpha)-\theta} + 0.25\Big)$

This gives about 1.6–2.7 events per second across the three clips.

7. Chords from pose harmony.py

This uses the filtered pixel positions $\mathbf p$ and torso length $\ell$ from §2. Let the hip centre be $\mathbf c = \tfrac12(\mathbf p_{\text{Lhip}}+\mathbf p_{\text{Rhip}})$. Each frame is tested against three rules, and the first match wins:

  • V, all down. Let $y_{\downarrow} = c_y + 0.5\,(\bar y_{\text{knee}} - c_y)$ (mid-thigh). The rule matches if $y_{\text{Lwr}} > y_\downarrow$ and $y_{\text{Rwr}} > y_\downarrow$ (remember $y$ points down).
  • ii, one side. Both wrists are left of $\min(x_{\text{Lank}},x_{\text{Rank}}) - 0.15\,\ell$, or both are right of $\max(x_{\text{Lank}},x_{\text{Rank}}) + 0.15\,\ell$.
  • I, at home. Take the body's own left–right axis $\mathbf u = (\mathbf p_{\text{Lhip}}-\mathbf p_{\text{Rhip}})/\|\cdot\|$ and the lateral coordinate $\lambda_j = (\mathbf p_j-\mathbf c)\cdot\mathbf u/\ell$ (positive means her left). The rule matches if $\lambda_{\text{Lwr}}>0.05,\ \lambda_{\text{Rwr}}<-0.05,\ \lambda_{\text{Lank}}>0.05,\ \lambda_{\text{Rank}}<-0.05$ and the hip width is $\|\mathbf p_{\text{Lhip}}-\mathbf p_{\text{Rhip}}\| > 0.25\,\ell$. When she is side-on the hip width collapses and the rule cannot fire. Because the axis is her own, it still works when she turns her back.
  • If none matches, or any of the six joints involved has confidence $c<0.5$, the reading is empty.

Dwell. The chord starts on I. It changes to a reading only after that reading has lasted 10 consecutive frames (0.35 s) unbroken. Empty readings never change it.

Altered V (demo ii–V–I, altered). While the chord is V, G13 becomes G7alt when $\widehat{\text{Weight}}$ is high. This uses hysteresis: on above 0.55, off below 0.4. Alterations shorter than 14 frames (0.47 s) are removed, and a short V or V-alt run at the edge of an alteration takes the colour of its neighbour.

Voicings are rootless, with the bass carrying the root (MIDI numbers, C4 = 60):

chordbassvoices

The largest move of any upper voice between any two of these chords is 2 semitones. ii→V moves one voice (C→B). V→I moves three voices down a step while B holds. G7alt is a semitone from both neighbours in every voice that moves.

8. Synthesis primitives synths.py

Control to audio. A frame-rate control $u_t$ is linearly interpolated to audio rate, then passed through a one-pole glide: $$y[n] = y[n-1] + \beta\,(x[n]-y[n-1]),\qquad \beta = 1-e^{-1/(\tau F_s)}.$$ Each mapping below gives its $\tau$. Pitches glide in semitones, $f = 440\cdot 2^{(m-69)/12}$ with $m$ glided, so every step sounds equal.

Oscillator phase $\varphi[n] = 2\pi\sum_{i\le n} f[i]/F_s$ (so pitch can vary smoothly without clicks). Band-limited saw, additive: $\operatorname{saw}_K(\varphi) = \frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\sin k\varphi}{k}$ (no aliasing).

Filter. A time-varying topology-preserving state-variable filter (Zavalishin), per sample: $g=\tan(\pi f_c/F_s)$, $k=1/Q$, $a_1 = 1/(1+g(g+k))$, $a_2=g a_1$, $a_3 = g a_2$; $v_3 = x - z_2$, $v_1 = a_1 z_1 + a_2 v_3$, $v_2 = z_2 + a_2 z_1 + a_3 v_3$, $z_1 \leftarrow 2v_1 - z_1$, $z_2 \leftarrow 2v_2 - z_2$. The low-pass output is $v_2$ and the band-pass output is $v_1$.

Plucked string (Karplus–Strong). Delay length $N=\lfloor 44100/f\rfloor$, filled with uniform noise that is smoothed by $\lfloor 3(1-b)\rfloor+1$ passes of a two-point average (brightness $b = 0.35+0.6\sigma$, so harder hits are brighter). Each step outputs $y=z[i]$ and writes back $z[i] \leftarrow 0.996\cdot\tfrac12(z[i]+z[i+1])$. It rings for 2.5 s.

FM. $\sin\!\big(\varphi_c + I\sin\varphi_m\big)$. The modulation index $I$ controls how many sidebands there are, and so how harsh it sounds.

Pan is equal power: $(\cos\frac{\pi p}{2},\,\sin\frac{\pi p}{2})\cdot x$, with $p$ = the joint's screen $x/W$ unless stated. Reverb: convolution with a synthetic impulse response, $n(t)e^{-t/0.35\,\text{s}}$ over 1.6 s, 6-tap smoothed, unit energy, independent noise per channel, wet 0.2. Master: sum the stems, add reverb, apply $g = 0.89/\text{peak}$, then $\tanh(1.1\,g\,x)/\tanh(1.1)$. The stems for the mixer are reverb·$g$ without the tanh, so the sliders at 100% reproduce the mix apart from the soft clip.

9. Demos

Notation: $\hat s_e$ is normalised limb speed, and $\widehat W, \widehat F, \hat S$ are normalised Weight, Flow and Space (§4). $\operatorname{g}_\tau(\cdot)$ is the glide of §8 with time constant $\tau$.

ii–V–I from pose

The chord comes from §7. Five stems:

  • pad (Space). Four voices at the voicing pitches $m_v$, glided with $\tau=0.25$ s, each detuned by $\pm 30\,w\,\sin(2\pi r_v t)$ cents, where $r_v = 0.31, 0.47, 0.71, 0.23$ Hz and $w=\operatorname{g}_{0.15}\big((1-\hat S)^{1.3}\big)$. Straight paths give $w\to0$ (in tune). Source $\operatorname{saw}_{10}$, then a 1600 Hz low-pass ($Q$ 0.7). Level $0.2\,\operatorname{g}_{0.06}(0.15+0.85\,h^{1.2})$, with $h=\operatorname{clip}(1.6\cdot\tfrac12(\hat s_{\text{Lwr}}+\hat s_{\text{Rwr}}),0,1)$. The 0.15 floor keeps the chord audible when she is still. Fixed pans 0.25, 0.75, 0.4, 0.6.
  • bass (Flow). FM with carrier = modulator = the root (glided with $\tau=0.12$ s), $I = \operatorname{g}_{0.06}(0.3+4\widehat F^{1.2})$, level $0.2\,\operatorname{g}_{0.06}\big(0.2+0.8\operatorname{clip}(1.3\widehat W,0,1)\big)$. Centred.
  • plucks (Time). At each sudden event (§6), the limb plucks its tone of the chord sounding at that frame: left ankle → root + 12, right ankle → voice 2, left wrist → voice 3 + 12, right wrist → voice 4 + 12. Gain $0.55(0.25+0.75\sigma)$, panned to the limb's screen position.
  • wind (Weight). White noise through the SVF with $f_c = \operatorname{g}_{0.05}(180\cdot2^{5.2\widehat W})$ Hz (180 Hz to about 6.5 kHz), $Q=1.2$, output $0.6\,\text{BP}+0.4\,\text{LP}$. Level $0.5\,\operatorname{g}_{0.05}\big(\operatorname{clip}(1.3\widehat W,0,1)^{0.9}\big)$, pan $\operatorname{g}_{0.25}(\widehat{\text{travel}})$.
  • wind tuned. The same noise through 8 band-pass resonators ($Q=60$) at each voice + 12 and + 24 semitones (glided with $\tau = 0.25$ s), RMS-matched to the plain wind, with the same level and pan. Blending the two sliders moves between air and a whistled chord.

10. Live (camera) web/live/features.js · harmony.js · audio.js

The live tool computes the same quantities in the browser, one camera frame at a time. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (tasks-vision 1.0.1, VIDEO mode, GPU delegate with CPU fallback, one person, confidences 0.5) runs on every new frame. Everything above looks both ways in time (zero-phase filters, centred windows). Live, only the past exists, so each step has a causal counterpart. Time steps $\Delta t$ are measured, not assumed, because a camera does not keep an exact rate.

One Euro. For a coordinate $x$, with $\alpha(f_c) = 1 - e^{-2\pi f_c \Delta t}$: $$\dot{\hat x} \leftarrow \dot{\hat x} + \alpha(f_d)\Big(\frac{x-\hat x}{\Delta t} - \dot{\hat x}\Big),\qquad f_c = f_{\min} + \beta\,|\dot{\hat x}|,\qquad \hat x \leftarrow \hat x + \alpha(f_c)(x - \hat x)$$ with $f_{\min} = 1.2$ Hz (the Smoothing slider sets $f_{\min} = 4.3 - s$), $\beta = 0.004$ per px/s and $f_d = 1$ Hz. A slow joint gets a low cutoff (jitter removed). A fast joint gets a high one (little lag).

Trailing Savitzky–Golay. Over the last $w$ seconds of filtered positions, with $\tau_i = t_i - t$, fit $\hat x(\tau) = \sum_{q\le d} c_q \tau^q$ by least squares and take $x^{(k)}(t) = k!\,c_k$: velocity $(w,d) = (0.23\,\text{s}, 2)$, acceleration $(0.3\,\text{s}, 2)$, jerk $(0.5\,\text{s}, 3)$.

Windows. Weight: $\sqrt{\max_{0.25\,\text{s}} E}$ then a one-pole glide with $\tau = 0.06$ s. Time and Flow: trailing means over 0.25 s. Space: the last 0.7 s, then $\tau = 0.08$ s. Sensitivity sliders multiply the normalised value (Space is scaled about its midpoint, 0.5).

Sudden events. A Schmitt trigger on each limb's $\alpha_e$. The trigger arms when $\alpha_e < 0.5\theta$, tracks the peak once $\alpha_e > \theta$, and fires on the first frame that $\alpha_e$ falls, at least 0.23 s after that limb's last event. $\theta = 18.4$ torsos/s², divided by the Suddenness slider. Strength is as in §6.

Chords follow the rules of §7, with the dwell (0.35 s) and the altered-V minimum (0.5 s) measured in seconds.

Sound uses Web Audio nodes with the parameters of §9 (ii–V–I demo):

  • Every control reaches its parameter through setTargetAtTime(value, now, τ). That is the one-pole glide of §8 with the same $\tau$.
  • Pitches are set on a 440 Hz base through detune $=100(m-69)$ cents, so they glide in semitones as offline.
  • Saws are the browser's band-limited sawtooth. Filters are RBJ biquads rather than the TPT state-variable filter; a low-pass $Q$ is given in dB (−3 dB ≈ 0.707).
  • FM: the modulator drives the carrier's frequency with deviation $I\cdot440$. That deviation is scaled by $2^{\text{detune}/1200}$ like the base, so it is $I f$ at any pitch, which is equivalent to the phase modulation $\sin(\varphi + I\sin\varphi)$.
  • Tuned wind: 8 biquad band-passes, $Q=60$, with a fixed ×4 make-up gain in place of offline RMS matching.
  • Plucks: Karplus–Strong computed in JavaScript per event, as in §8.
  • Reverb: the same impulse recipe, dry 0.8 / wet 0.2. A limiter (−6 dB, 20:1) protects the output.
  • When nobody is in frame, all levels glide to 0 (pad $\tau=0.6$ s, the rest 0.3 s).

Validation. The live feature code was run on the same landmark files as the offline pipeline and compared:

  • Mean levels agree (for example, Weight 0.20 vs 0.25).
  • Allowing for lag, the correlations are: Weight $r\approx 0.8$ at 6–8 frames, Time 0.77–0.89 at 11–12 frames, Flow 0.72–0.84 and Space ≈ 0.7 at about 15 frames, hand speed 0.74–0.79 at 2 frames.
  • Sudden events: 285 live vs 292 offline on the longest clip. 83% of them are within 0.4 s of an offline event, and they fire a median of 67 ms after the offline peak.

Part of the measured lag is the offline centred windows responding early. The lag relative to the movement itself is smaller. Flow and Space are the slowest, but they only colour the bass and the pad's tuning.

Total latency on stage is roughly camera (30–50 ms) + pose (15–30 ms on a laptop GPU) + feature lag + audio output (the page shows its measured fps, pose time and audio latency).
